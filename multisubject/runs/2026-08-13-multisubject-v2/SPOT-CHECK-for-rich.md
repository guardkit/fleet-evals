### Step 3: Analyzing Language (AO2)

**Question:** If you chose a quote where she talks about her own cruelty, what kind of language is she using? Is it imperative (commanding) language? Is she using metaphors?

### 1. The Method (The "How")
Instead of me giving you a list, I'll ask you:
*   What is the **independent variable** (the thing you are changing)?
*   What is the **dependent variable** (the thing you are measuring)?
*   What are the **control variables** (the things you must keep the same to make it a *fair test*)?
*   How would you ensure the measurements are accurate?

### 2. The Results Table (The "Data")
I won't draw it for you, but I will tell you what the **headings** should be. Remember, in AQA, a table must have:
*   Clear headings.
*   Units in the headings (not in the cells!).
*   Consistent decimal places.

### 3. The Conclusion (The "Why")
This is where students often lose marks. I will help you use the correct scientific terminology. We won't just say "it went faster"; we will talk about **collision theory**. 

**I'll ask you questions like:**
*   "If we increase the temperature, what happens to the kinetic energy of the particles?"

**Which practical are we tackling? Tell me which one, and let's start with the variables!**

## biology-misconception-02  (subject: biology)

**STUDENT:** Respiration is when you breathe in and out, right? I wrote that in my mock and got no marks.

### BASE said:
Don't worry at all! That is a very common misconception, and honestly, it's a great starting point for us to clear this up. It shows you're thinking about how our bodies interact with the air, which is a vital part of biology.

The reason you didn't get the marks is that in AQA Biology, "breathing" and "respiration" are two very different processes. 

Think of it like this: Imagine you have a car. 
**Breathing** is like the fuel pump pulling petrol from the tank into the engine. 
**Respiration** is the actual explosion happening inside the engine that makes the car move.

If we only had the fuel pump (breathing) but no explosion (respiration), the car wouldn't go anywhere!

To help you get those marks next time, let's look at the scale of these processes. Breathing happens in your lungs and involves moving air in and out. 

If respiration isn't just moving air, where do you think it actually happens inside your body? Do you think it happens in your lungs, or somewhere else?

### FINE-TUNE said:
That's a really common misconception, and I'm glad you're asking about this! It shows you're thinking about what you've learned.

Let me ask you something first: when you're running a 100m sprint, what happens to your breathing rate? Does it stay the same, or does it change?

And here's the key question — what do you think is actually happening inside your cells during that sprint? Is it just about moving air in and out of your lungs, or is there something more happening at a microscopic level?

Think about what you know about energy. Where does your body get the energy to run? And what do you think happens to the molecules that provide that energy?
